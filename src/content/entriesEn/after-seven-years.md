| Contingency (about 10%–15%) | ¥200k–350k |
| **Per episode / season total** | **about ¥1.8m–3.15m** |

### Total budget

- **Pilot stage (3 years)**: about ¥600k–1m for first visits to 3–5 families, short follow-ups and method validation.
- **First season production**: about ¥1.8m–3.15m, the per-episode budget above.
- **Long-term project (about 8–9 episodes)**: in today’s purchasing power, about ¥15m–30m, including the pilot and later production, excluding inflation and distribution.

A long-term project should not raise all fifty years of funding at once. A more realistic path is to finish the pilot and first season first, then build funding season by season through staged films, grants, platform partnerships, documentary festivals and long-term sponsors; re-budget before each new season starts every seven years.

## 1. Inspiration

This project is inspired by the British documentary series *Up*.

The series began in 1964, directed by Michael Apted and Paul Almond and produced by ITV. The directors chose 14 seven-year-old children from different social classes in Britain and revisited them every seven years to record their lives. The original intent was to test the hypothesis of fixed British class structure — the famous saying “Give me a child until he is seven, and I will give you the man” is the starting point of the project.

From 1964 to 2019 the series spanned 56 years, from age 7 to age 63, with the latest episode *63 Up* airing in 2019. On 7 January 2021, director Michael Apted died at 79. The next instalment, *70 Up*, is scheduled to air within 2026.

In 2005 Channel 4 included the series in its list of the 50 greatest documentaries.

The value of the series is not only that it confirmed class exists, but that it used time itself to answer a larger question: **how a person becomes himself in the river of his times.** That is the starting point of this project.

## 2. Overview

### One-line definition

A long-term documentary project that tracks the changing lives of ordinary Chinese people to record a period of dramatic social transformation and shifting values.

### Positioning

- **Region**: China
- **Duration**: over 50 years
- **Format**: documentary series, one season every 7 years
- **Core question**: in a country undergoing dramatic change, how do individuals grow, drift, choose and get chosen

## 3. Core ideas

| Principle | Description |
| --- | --- |
| No pursuit of stability | People moving, clashing ideas, and the collapse and rebuilding of value systems are all core subjects |
| Refusal is material | Dropping out, refusing to be filmed or staying silent are all part of the work; the gap is kept |
| No preset standard | The director does not judge “success” or “failure”, only presents the traces of time |
| Dynamic recording | If a subject goes through a major event, such as migration or a serious upheaval, extra recording is triggered |

## 4. Method

### 4.1 Rhythm

- **Main rhythm**: one season every 7 years, each focusing on about 2 weeks of intensive recording
- **Trigger**: extra shooting is triggered when a subject goes through a major event
- **Forms**: interview (fixed questions + variable questions) + life follow-along + self-narration (vlog diary)

### 4.2 Fixed question bank (asked every season)

The questions borrow from the fixed questions of *Up* each season, localized for the Chinese context:

1. What do you think “success” is?
2. How do you imagine the next 10 years?
3. Are you happy now? Why?
4. Where do you feel is “home”?
5. What has been your biggest change in the past 7 years?
6. If you could say one sentence to yourself 7 years ago, what would it be?

### 4.3 Director’s notes

After each season, the director writes a set of Director’s Notes recording:

- Changes in the subjects’ state
- The limits and confusions of the director’s own understanding
- Observations of the background of the times

These notes are kept as supplementary text for the film.

### 4.4 Iteration

This plan is reviewed and iterated at the following points:

- Before each new season starts
- Every 7 years as subjects age
- After major social events
- When the director’s own understanding changes significantly

Each iteration records what changed and when, as part of the project’s history.

## 5. Sample concept

### 5.1 Selection principles

- **Multiple facets**: reflect the richness of Chinese society rather than a single class
- **Expected change**: prioritize families likely to experience strong geographic or class mobility
- **Fault tolerance**: recruit 8–10 families initially, aiming to track at least 7 stably
- **Upper limit**: no more than 14, following the original *Up* sample size while keeping depth

### 5.2 Initial outline

The initial pool does not need to cover every type at once. Build a pool of 8–10 families first, then fix the first-season list based on real contact, willingness and long-term reachability.

| Code | Birth era | Background | Expected tension |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 2000s | Western rural area / left-behind child / student loan / works in a tier-1 city | Rural-urban drift, the cost of class crossing |
| B | 2010s | Eastern city middle class / international education / plan to go abroad | Identity and belonging as globalization recedes |
| C | 2020s | Urban-rural fringe / parents return home to start a business | The next generation redefining “stability” and “risk” |

> Note: this is an initial concept. Real contact will shape it, focusing on samples that better show contemporary China’s changes.

## 6. Ethical boundaries

### 6.1 Dynamic consent

Written consent is obtained again before each season. Participants can adjust their involvement at any stage, for example “this year only a voice interview, no face”.

### 6.2 Freedom to leave

Anyone may leave at any stage without explanation. The time and reason are recorded as part of the “refusal is material” principle.

### 6.3 Privacy protection

Content involving major privacy requires a separate authorization agreement. For child participants, dual consent is required from both parents and the child (depending on age).

### 6.4 Director self-reflection

The director keeps an honest record of his own confusion and generational distance in the film, without pretending to an “all-knowing view”.

### 6.5 Ethical experience and responses

| Issue | Reference experience | Response here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Participants being labelled by the public | Participants may be misunderstood because of their mental state | Keep communicating, respect their right to define their own life |
| Dropping out mid-way | Participants may return years later | Treat leaving as part of the story, record the reason, keep the door open |
| Pressure from filming | Filming may force people to revisit the past they avoid | Provide mental-health resources, stress “no probing, no forcing” |

## 7. Execution path

### Stage one: pilot (target: within 3 years)

- Find 3–5 families willing to take part in the pilot
- Complete an initial interview of about 1–2 hours per person
- Validate the question framework
- Assess feasibility and the direction of adjustment

### Stage two: official start (target: first season within 5 years)

- Fix the final participant list (3–5 people)
- Complete the first season’s core shooting (about 2 weeks per person)
- Finish editing and approach platforms
- Build long-term contact and tracking

### Stage three: long-term run (50 years)

- Release one season every 7 years
- Keep in regular contact with participants (a greeting once a year)
- Continuously iterate the question framework and filming approach

## 8. Risks and responses

| Risk | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Funding breaks down | Staged output (a film per season) to attract platform investment and rights revenue |
| Participants drop out | “Refusal is material” principle; keep the gap as part of the narrative |
| The director cannot last 50 years | Build a “director succession mechanism”, train successors, keep the core spirit |
| Social change exceeds expectations | Make “change” itself the main line; do not box content into a preset framework |
| Public opinion pressure | Protect anonymity, use pseudonyms when needed, check in on mental state regularly |

## 9. Vision

### Short term (every 7 years)

Produce one season of about 90 minutes per episode, showing the subjects’ lives now and the background of the times.

### Ultimate (after 50 years)

Form a **people’s visual history** of China’s social change, so that future people can understand, through ordinary eyes, how this era was lived, understood and crossed.

### Core belief

> Instability itself is worth recording.
>
> China has gone through dramatic social change in the past 50 years, and this “sense of drift” is exactly the most valuable coordinate for this film. People moving, clashing ideas, and value systems turning over are all traces of time.
