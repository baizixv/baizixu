## About the project

Human Benchmark is a collection of online cognitive tests. Through a series of short brain games it helps you quickly gauge your reaction speed, short-term memory, visual memory, attention and hand-eye coordination.

It works best as a lightweight tool for self-tracking and comparison, not as medical diagnosis or rigorous psychometrics. Results are affected by device refresh rate, input latency, browser performance, network conditions and how much you have practised.

## Tests

The current page includes the following tests:

- **Reaction test**: measure visual reaction time.
- **Number memory**: remember increasingly long digit sequences.
- **Chimp test**: watch and remember the positions of numbered tiles.
- **Typing test**: measure words per minute.
- **Visual memory**: remember an increasing number of tile positions.
- **Aim test**: click targets as fast as you can, testing speed and accuracy.
- **Color test**: quickly find the tile with a different color.
- **Stroop test**: judge the relationship between words and colors.
- **Verbal memory**: remember as many words as possible.
- **Sequence memory**: remember increasingly long visual sequences.
- **Luck test**: relax with a light random test.
- **Speed test**: measure the speed of repeated actions.
- **Time perception**: try to judge a time interval.
- **Hand-eye coordination**: watch how hand input and visual targets work together.
- **Calculation test**: finish calculations quickly and accurately.
- **Schulte grid**: train number search and concentration.
- **Dynamic visual acuity**: test sensitivity to moving objects.
- **Attention test**: see how long you can stay focused.

## How to use it

Keep your device and environment consistent, and treat results as a personal record rather than an absolute comparison with others. Test in a quiet place with a stable network and normal device performance, and keep your posture, viewing distance and habits the same.

To watch training effects, record a result every so often and look at your trend rather than a single personal best.

## FAQ

### What reaction time is normal?

Most people’s visual reaction time falls between 200 and 300 milliseconds, but results depend on the device, network, screen refresh rate and practice.

### How many digits can people usually remember?

Many people can reliably hold around 7 digits in short-term memory, and training usually pushes that number higher.

### Are phone and computer results the same?

Not necessarily. Screen refresh rate, touch latency, input method and browser performance all affect performance, so compare on a fixed device.

### How do I make results more stable?

Test with a stable network, a quiet environment and normal device performance, and keep posture, distance and habits as consistent as possible.
